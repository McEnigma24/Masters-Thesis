Title: Application of machine learning algorithms for physical layer optimization and link adaptation mechanisms in 5G/6G networks.

Topic description: The thesis examines the feasibility and cost-effectiveness of machine learning (ML) algorithms that replace conventional signal processing in modern telecommunications networks. The research focuses on cross-layer optimization at the boundary between the physical layer (PHY) and link adaptation mechanisms. The work will analyze selected elements of an innovative telecom stack: neural receivers, techniques for compressing and predicting channel state information (CSI), and modulation and coding scheme (MCS) selection algorithms that leverage this information.

Motivation: The motivation for undertaking this topic is a desire to gain an in-depth understanding of telecommunications systems and to train, deploy, and compare different neural network architectures that replace classical processing. Given stringent real-time constraints, models will undergo quantization, distillation, and pruning to bring their performance closer to deployable operation in realistic scenarios. The studies will evaluate diverse architectures: autoencoders, convolutional neural networks (CNNs), transformers, and recurrent networks (LSTM/GRU). Because inference latency in radio networks often must not exceed fractions of a millisecond, designing hardware-aware ML solutions becomes essential, including neural architecture search (NAS) under latency constraints, network pruning, and parameter quantization.

Measurable goals: (1) comparing reception quality (BLER/BER vs SNR) between a classical receiver and neural variants in link-level simulation (Sionna PHY); (2) assessing the quality--complexity--latency trade-off for selected architectures; (3) analyzing model generalization beyond training conditions (channel, SNR, mobility, MCS); (4) studying the impact of CSI compression and prediction on link adaptation effectiveness in system-level simulation (Sionna SYS); (5) integrating selected models with the OpenAirInterface stack via ONNX export and real-time inference (ONNX Runtime / TensorRT), with end-to-end validation of latency and quality.
